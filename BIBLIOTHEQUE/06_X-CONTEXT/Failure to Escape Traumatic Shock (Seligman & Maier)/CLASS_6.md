# Failure to Escape Traumatic Shock (1967)
Source: Failure to Escape Traumatic Shock (Seligman & Maier).md

## Core reasons
- The paper reports behavioral experiments in dogs on escapable vs. inescapable shock and learned helplessness, a psychology topic rather than ML model design.
- It provides cross-disciplinary conceptual context about learning/behavior (learned helplessness) rather than datasets, benchmarks, or ML mechanisms.

## Evidence extracts
- "These results were interpreted as supporting a learned \"helplessness\" explanation of interference with escape responding: Ss failed to escape shock in the shuttle box following inescapable shock in the harness because they had learned that shock termination was independent of responding." (Opening paragraph)
- "Learning that one's own responding and reinforcement are independent might be expected to play a role in appetitive situations." (General Discussion)
