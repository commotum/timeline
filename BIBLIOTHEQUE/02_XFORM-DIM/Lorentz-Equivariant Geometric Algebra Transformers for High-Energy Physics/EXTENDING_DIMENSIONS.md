## 2. One-Sentence Contribution Summary

The paper proposes L-GATr, “a new multipurpose architecture for high-energy physics” that is Lorentz-equivariant and applied to regression, classification, and generative modeling tasks (Abstract).

## 3. Tasks Evaluated

- Task name: Surrogates for QFT amplitudes (amplitude regression)
  - Task type: Other (regression)
  - Dataset(s) used: MadGraph-generated phase space inputs and amplitudes for $q\bar{q}\to Z+ng$ with $n=1,\ldots,4$
  - Domain: High-energy physics / particle physics (four-momenta, scattering amplitudes)
  - Evidence: “We first demonstrate L-GATr as a neural surrogate for quantum field theoretical amplitudes [5–7, 57, 58]” (Section 4.1 Surrogates for QFT amplitudes). “As example processes, we study  $q\bar{q}\to Z+ng$ , the production of a Z boson with  $n=1,\ldots,4$  additional gluons from a quark-antiquark pair. For each gluon multiplicity, we train a L-GATr model to predict the amplitude as a function of the four-momenta of the initial and final particles.” (Section 4.1 Surrogates for QFT amplitudes). “We generate training and evaluation data consisting of phase space inputs and their corresponding interaction amplitudes for processes  $q\bar{q}\to Z+ng,\,n=\{1,4\}$ , where an initial quark-antiquark pair interact to produce a Z boson and a variable number of gluons.” (Appendix C.1 Dataset). “These datasets are generated by the MadGraph Monte Carlo event generator [3] in two steps.” (Appendix C.1 Dataset).

- Task name: Top tagging
  - Task type: Classification
  - Dataset(s) used: Kasieczka et al. top tagging dataset; detector-level ATLAS-simulated events
  - Domain: High-energy physics / particle physics (reconstructed hadrons / detector-level point clouds)
  - Evidence: “Next, we turn to the problem of classifying whether a spray of reconstructed hadrons originated from the decay of a top quark or any other process.” (Section 4.2 Top tagging). “We use the established top tagging dataset by Kasieczka et al. [48, 49] as a benchmark” (Section 4.2 Top tagging). “The data samples are structured as point clouds, with each event simulating a measurement by the ATLAS experiment at detector level. Signal samples originate from the decay of a top quark, while the rest of the events are generated by standard background processes.” (Appendix C.2 Dataset).

- Task name: Generative modelling of reconstructed events
  - Task type: Generation
  - Dataset(s) used: $pp \to t\bar{t}+n$ jets (n=0...4) simulated with MadGraph/Pythia/Delphes; reconstructed detector-level events
  - Domain: High-energy physics / particle physics (reconstructed particles, event generation)
  - Evidence: “Finally, we study the generative modelling of reconstructed events as an end-to-end generation task [16, 17], bypassing the whole simulation chain visualized in Fig. 1.” (Section 4.3 Generative modelling). “We focus on the processes  $pp \to t\bar{t} + n$  jets, the generation of top pairs with  $n = 0 \dots 4$  additional jets, where the top quarks decay hadronically,  $t \to bq'\bar{q}''$ .” (Section 4.3 Generative modelling). “Dataset The  $t\bar{t}+n$  jets, n=0...4 dataset is simulated with the MadGraph 3.5.1 [3] event generation toolchain, consisting of MadEvent [3] for the underlying hard process, Pythia 8 [70] for the parton shower, Delphes 3 [34] for a fast detector simulation, and the anti- $k_T$  jet reconstruction algorithm [20] with R=0.4 as implemented in FASTJET [21].” (Appendix C.3 Generative modelling).

## 6. Input and Representation Constraints

- Variable-sized inputs (no fixed number of tokens): “The inputs, hidden states, and outputs of L-GATr are variablesize sets of tokens.” (Section 3.1)
- Token structure and fixed representation: “Each token consists of n copies of the geometric algebra  $\mathbb{G}_{1,3}$  and m additional scalar channels.” (Section 3.1). “We represent each particle as a token, store the particle type as a one-hot embedding in the scalar channels and the four-momentum in the first grade of the geometric algebra.” (Section 3.1)
- Variable-length inputs explicitly supported: “It supports variable-length inputs, as found in many LHC problems, and even large models can be trained efficiently.” (Introduction)
- Additional fixed global token for regression: “In addition to the particle tokens we use one "global" token, initialized to zero.” (Appendix C.1 Models)
- Fixed 4D Minkowski representation and coordinate constraints for generative modeling: “The L-GATr score network operates in Minkowski space  $p = (E, p_x, p_y, p_z)$ , whereas flow matching happens in the physically motivated coordinates  $y = (y_m, y_p, \eta, \phi)$  defined in Eq. (4).” (Appendix C.3 Models)
- Explicit kinematic constraints / cuts: “reconstructed particles  $p=(E,\vec{p})$  are often required to satisfy constraints of the form  $p_1^2+p_2^2\geq p_{T\,\text{min}}^2$  and  $p^2>0$ .” (Section 3.2). “Our event sets feature kinematic cuts in the transverse momentum of the outgoing particles ( $p_T>20~{\rm GeV}$ ) and on the angular distance between the gluons ( $\Delta R=\sqrt{\Delta\eta^2+\Delta\phi^2}>0.4$ ).” (Appendix C.1 Dataset). “apply the standard phase space cuts  $p_T>20~{\rm GeV}$ ,  $\Delta R=\sqrt{\Delta\phi^2+\Delta\eta^2}<0.4$  and require 2 b-tagged jets.” (Appendix C.3 Dataset)
- Ordering constraint for reconstructed particles: “identical particles are ordered by  $p_T$ .” (Appendix C.3 Dataset)
- Input preprocessing constraints: “We standardize the four-momentum inputs to unit variance, using the same normalization for each component in order to not break Lorentz equivariance.” (Appendix C.1 Models). “We rescale the four-momenta p by the standard deviation of all five datasets  $206.6~{\rm GeV}$  for processing with neural networks.” (Appendix C.3 Dataset)
- Fixed patch size / padding / resizing: Not specified.

## 11. Architectural Workarounds

- Scaled dot-product attention with optimized backend for scaling: “Because it computes pairwise interactions through scaled dot-product attention, for which there are highly optimized backends like Flash Attention [32], the architecture scales particularly well to problems with many tokens or particles.” (Introduction)
- Symmetry-breaking inputs to handle detector-induced violations: “L-GATr supports such broken or approximate symmetries by including the symmetry-breaking effects as additional inputs into the network. Concretely, whenever we analyze reconstruction-level data, we include the beam directions; see Appendix C.” (Section 3.1)
- Riemannian flow matching and manifold parameterization to enforce constraints: “we parameterize the reachable four-momentum space for each particle as a manifold and use geodesics on this manifold as probability paths from base samples to data points.” (Section 3.2). “we can even hard-code phase-space boundaries, which are commonplace in high-energy physics.” (Section 3.2)
- Task-specific pooling token for regression: “In addition to the particle tokens we use one "global" token, initialized to zero.” (Appendix C.1 Models)
- Extra symmetry-breaking multivector channels in generative model: “We add extra multivector channels as symmetry-breaking inputs, in particular the time direction (1,0,0,0) as a rank-1 multivector, and the plane orthogonal to the beam direction encoded as a rank-2 multivector.” (Appendix C.3 Models)

## 12. Explicit Limitations and Non-Claims

- Computational overhead limitation: “Still, L-GATr has its limitations. While the architecture scales better than comparable message-passing networks, it has some computational overhead compared to, for instance, efficient Transformer implementations.” (Section 5 Discussion)
- Foundation model non-claim: “While L-GATr should in principle be suitable for pretraining across multiple problems, we have not yet investigated its potential as a foundation model.” (Section 5 Discussion)
- Generative modeling limitation: “However, none of the models are able to capture the kinematics of the top mass peak at percent-level precision yet.” (Section 4.3 Kinematic distributions)
- Efficiency limitation for small systems: “L-GATr in its current implementation is not yet as efficient as a Transformer for small systems: for up to hundreds of particles, L-GATr takes an order of magnitude longer to evaluate.” (Appendix C.4 Computational cost and scalability)

### 14. Final Classification

**Multi-task, single-domain.** The paper evaluates “three particle-physics applications” and explicitly sequences separate tasks: “We demonstrate L-GATr on three particle-physics applications. We first train neural surrogates for quantum field theoretic amplitudes, a regression problem with high demands on precision. Next, we train classification models and evaluate L-GATr on the popular benchmark problem of top tagging. Finally, we turn to the generative modelling of reconstructed particles, which can make the entire analysis pipeline substantially more efficient.” (Introduction). All evaluations are within high-energy/particle physics, so the scope is multi-task but not multi-domain.
