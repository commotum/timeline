## 2. One-Sentence Contribution Summary

The paper claims to solve multi-view novel view synthesis from sparse context by proposing a 2D-only transformer pipeline: "We propose a 2D-only method that maps multiple context views and a query pose to a new image in a single pass of a neural network." (Abstract)

## 4. Domain and Modality Scope

- Single domain? No; multiple domains within the same modality: "We evaluate our approach on both real and synthetic datasets: a) **Shepard-Metzler-7-Parts** (SM7) [21,66] is a synthetic dataset, where objects composed of 7 cubes of different colors are rotated in space. b) **ShapeNet** [13] is a synthetic dataset of simple objects. We use  $128 \times 128$  pixel images rendered by [69] containing two categories: cars and chairs. c) **InteriorNet** [35] is a collection of interior environments designed by 1,100 professional designers. We used the publicly available part of the dataset (20k scenes with 20 images each). While the dataset is synthetic, the renderings are similar to real-world environments. The first 600 environments serve as our test set." (4 Experiments); "**3D (CO3D)** [56] is a real-world dataset containing 1.5 million images showing almost 19k objects from 51 MS-COCO [38] categories (*e.g.*, apple, donut, vase, etc.)." (4 Experiments); "e) **7-Scenes** [24] is a real-world dataset depicting 7 indoor scenes as captured by a Kinect RGB-D camera." (4 Experiments).
- Multiple modalities? Not claimed; the task is image-based with pose metadata: "Image-based novel view synthesis, *i.e.*, rendering a 3D scene from a novel view-point given a set of context views (images and camera poses)" (1 Introduction).
- Domain generalization / cross-domain transfer: Claimed; "In order to show that we can transfer a model pretrained on synthetic data to real-world scenes, we evaluate our method on the CO3D dataset [56]." (4 Experiments); "We train our model on classes of similar 3D scenes to be able to generalize to a novel scene with only a handful of images" (5 Conclusions & future work).

## 6. Input and Representation Constraints

- Fixed 2D image assumption: "We propose a 2D-only method that maps multiple context views and a query pose to a new image" (Abstract) and "It operates in 2D space without any explicit 3D reasoning" (Fig. 1).
- Fixed image-to-token grid: "The encoder first reduces the dimension of the input image from  $128 \times 128$  pixels to  $8 \times 8$  tokens by several strided convolution layers." (3 Method); "we use only  $8\times 8$  codes to represent an image." (4 Experiments).
- Tokenized representation: "In the code space, each image is represented by a sequence of tokens." (3 Method).
- Resizing / variable resolution across experiments: "As our method uses images of sizes  $128 \times 128$  pixels, we rescaled the images before training the codebook. For evaluation, we used the original image size  $64 \times 64$  pixels of the dataset." (Supplementary material); "The codebooks were evaluated with image size  $128 \times 128$ , except for 'CO3D@400', which was evaluated with image size  $400 \times 400$  pixels" (Table 6).
- Fixed patch size: Not specified.

## 7. Context Window and Attention Structure

- Maximum sequence length: Not specified; sequence length varies with number of views and latent token grid: "Each training batch consists of a set of n views" and "All  $K^{(i)}$ ,  $Q^{(i)}$ ,  $V^{(i)}$  have the size  $(nk^2)\times d_m$  where  $d_m$  is the dimensionality of the model and k is the size of the image in the latent space." (3 Method).
- Attention type: Branching attention with causal block attention and global access to previous images: "branching attention which computes attention over the tree shown in Fig. 3" and "we allow the model to attend to all previous images and all other vectors from the same image." (3 Method).
- Computational cost mechanisms: "This enables the codebook to focus on finer details in images while the transformer operates on shorter input sequences, reducing the quadratic memory complexity of its attention layer." (1 Introduction); "we propose a novel attention mechanism called branching attention with constant overhead regardless of how many query views we optimize." (1 Introduction).

## 10. Evidence of Constraint Masking

- Model size: "The architecture of our **transformer model** is based on GPT2-base [51], and has 12 transformer blocks, 12 attention heads, and the hidden size is 768." (Training details).
- Dataset size(s): "**InteriorNet** [35] is a collection of interior environments designed by 1,100 professional designers. We used the publicly available part of the dataset (20k scenes with 20 images each)." (4 Experiments); "**3D (CO3D)** [56] is a real-world dataset containing 1.5 million images showing almost 19k objects from 51 MS-COCO [38] categories" (4 Experiments).
- Evidence of scaling data helping: "The results show that our model has a large capacity (it is able to learn all categories while the baselines are only trained on a single category), and it benefits from more training data as can be seen when comparing models trained on 10 and all categories." (4 Experiments).
- Data requirement as a limitation: "One limitation of our approach is the large amount of data needed, which we tackle through pre-training on a large synthetic dataset." (5 Conclusions & future work).
- Training scale noted: "training our codebook took 480 GPU-hours, training the transformer on InteriorNet took 280 GPU-hours, and fine-tuning the transformer took 90 GPU-hours, giving a total of 850 GPU-hours." (4 Experiments).

## 11. Architectural Workarounds

- Two-stage codebook + transformer to reduce sequence length and attention cost: "Our model uses a two-stage architecture consisting of a Vector Quantized-Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) codebook [49] and a transformer model." (1 Introduction); "This enables the codebook to focus on finer details in images while the transformer operates on shorter input sequences, reducing the quadratic memory complexity of its attention layer." (1 Introduction).
- Branching attention to handle multiple context sizes efficiently: "we propose a novel attention mechanism called branching attention with constant overhead regardless of how many query views we optimize." (1 Introduction); "branching attention which computes attention over the tree shown in Fig. 3, and allows us to optimize the transformer model for all context sizes and tasks very efficiently." (3 Method).
- Mask-token strategy for single-pass decoding: "we need to replace the tokens corresponding to each query view with mask tokens to allow the transformer to decode them in a single forward pass." (3 Method).
- Task-specific head for localization: "generates the camera pose using a regression head attached to the output of the transformer corresponding to the query image tokens." (3 Method).
- Fixed latent grid as structural constraint: "The encoder first reduces the dimension of the input image from  $128 \times 128$  pixels to  $8 \times 8$  tokens" (3 Method).

## 12. Explicit Limitations and Non-Claims

- Localization not yet SOTA: "Even though the localization results are not yet competitive with state-of-the-art localization pipelines" (1 Introduction).
- Comparative weakness vs iterative localization: "Our approach performs similar to existing APR and RPR techniques that also use only a single forward pass in a network [1,8,32,65], but worse than iterative approaches such as [19]" (4 Experiments).
- Data requirement: "One limitation of our approach is the large amount of data needed" (5 Conclusions & future work).
- Need for fine-tuning: "we need to fine-tune both the codebook and the transformer to achieve high-quality results on new datasets" (5 Conclusions & future work).
- Scope of 3D reasoning: "It operates in 2D space without any explicit 3D reasoning" (Fig. 1).
- Future work scope: "We also want to experiment with a simpler architecture with no codebook and larger scenes, possibly of outdoor environments." (5 Conclusions & future work).

### 13. Constraint Profile (Synthesis)

> **Constraint Profile:**
> – Domain scope: multiple datasets spanning synthetic objects/indoor scenes and real-world objects/indoor scenes, all image-based.
> – Task structure: two main tasks (novel view synthesis + camera pose estimation) plus a codebook reconstruction evaluation.
> – Representation rigidity: fixed 2D image-to-8x8 token grid; resizing to 128x128/64x64/400x400 across experiments.
> – Model sharing vs specialization: shared transformer across tasks with task-specific heads; dataset-specific fine-tuning from InteriorNet.
> – Role of positional encoding: fixed trainable absolute embeddings shared across images; no PE variants compared.

### 14. Final Classification

**Multi-task, multi-domain (constrained)**. The model is explicitly used for two tasks—novel view synthesis and camera pose estimation—since it "can both render a novel view from a given pose and predict the pose for a given image" (1 Introduction), and evaluation spans "both real and synthetic datasets" (4 Experiments). The tasks and datasets are pre-defined and bounded, so the setup is multi-task and multi-domain but constrained rather than unrestrained.
